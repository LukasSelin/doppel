Reading the map

Every region is a package, and its area is its share of the functions — the map is a cartogram, not a floor plan. A region is tinted by the concept most of its functions carry.

A shared border is geometry; its weight is the data. Regions are packed so related ones tend to land side by side, but touching is not itself a claim. Where two related regions do meet, the border between them is painted by how much duplication crosses it. Where they don't, a curved arc says so instead — so nothing is hidden by the packing.

Reading this screen

The map asks where duplication sits; this screen settles one case. Pick a function and you get every other function scored against it, then the whole evidence for whichever of those pairs you select — enough to decide merge these or leave them.

The two scores are deliberately not blended. Code-shape is how alike the two bodies are; overlap is how much architectural context they share. High shape with low overlap is not a weaker finding, it is a different one — lookalike bodies in unrelated subsystems, which is rarely something to merge.
