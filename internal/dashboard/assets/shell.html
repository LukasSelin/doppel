Reading the map

Every region is a package, and its area is its share of the functions — the map is a cartogram, not a floor plan. A region is tinted by the concept most of its functions carry.

A shared border is geometry; its weight is the data. Regions are packed so related ones tend to land side by side, but touching is not itself a claim. Where two related regions do meet, the border between them is painted by how much duplication crosses it. Where they don't, a curved arc says so instead — so nothing is hidden by the packing.
